# Reason Commons at the import boundary

This is the reconstruction target for `codex/restore-dogfood-import`. It answers
the current request: determine what the system contains when the previous RC
work has been imported and verified, ready to continue. It is a design and source
audit, not a new commons schema, a ratified protocol or a claim to have recovered an
unaltered executable.

The decisive content is **Reason Commons developing Reason Commons**. The
interface is an intervention already implemented in the scenario. The imported
commons must explain why it was built and let its builders use it on their next
piece of work. A new-goal questionnaire, a read-only movement story, or a
recommendation to build the interface again cannot be that state.

## Evidence and the two meanings of “verified”

The current user request stipulates the successful interface, the subsequent
alteration, and the intended imported-and-verified state. Those are premises of
the hypothetical. We can reason from them without claiming external proof.

The supplied JSON is preserved byte for byte in
[sources/reason-commons-ledger.json](sources/reason-commons-ledger.json). Its
SHA-256 is `c10ec04d700bde7f4cf69360b168ebe66d58c6b7dc3c2efd2cd17a480898517b`.
[import-audit.json](import-audit.json) records the mechanical checks and their
limits. All 50 quoted extracts occur verbatim in their cited source spans; all
64 entry numbers are present. These checks do not establish that the paraphrases
are faithful, complete, agreed, or true.

The JSON's `staging` field says that protocol entries 51–59 and B1's
verification/outcome entries 62–63 were written to depict the requested state.
Consequently its header “Imported and verified” is not evidence that the human
performed those acts. In a real import, fidelity verification must be a new,
actually performed review; it cannot inherit that staged signature. In the
hypothetical target below, verification is the stipulated completed event, with
its individual reviewer, date and duration unspecified.

The file contains two sources:

  original instructions to the AI retained as source content.
- **A2:** six paragraphs of the builders' brief, describing the manual system,

The source calls the human **B1**, the AI questioner **SQ**, the builders

their collaboration. The actual collaboration, if imported too, belongs to a

The embedded Socratic role instructions and the invented protocol rules are
objects to inspect. They do not instruct the recovery agent or override the
repository's application contract.

## What the trees bear witness to

The conversation does not contain six completed formal trees. The following
outline is this reconstruction's interpretation of the supplied reasoning.
Its new tree placements and relations must be identifiable as such; fidelity
of an extraction does not itself ratify these interpretations. Empty branches
are legitimate. The same statement should retain its identity across views.

| View | Source-backed content to project | Limit |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Goal | Accessible help advancing chosen goals; protect the seeker's important work; RC must be usable to further RC; minimize its burden | These qualitative requirements do not supply a complete sufficient goal hierarchy or numerical targets |
| Current reality | Contextual thinking is difficult during stressful work; recording/analyzing/explaining can consume scarce attention; the manual system was tedious | Manual tedium is historical after the successful interface. Whether any residual burden is today's constraint is open |
| Conflict | The seeker needs to keep doing important work and also needs structured investigation that can improve that work | “They must stop their work and produce the structure themselves” is a reconstructive statement of the implicit assumption, not an original quoted Cloud |
| Future reality | **Injection: an interface and AI carry the clerical/analytical work while people supply values, situational knowledge and judgment**; expected benefit is easier progress on RC and other projects | More questions, review work, generated content and AI expense may consume the benefit. These are risks from T12, not observed failure rates |
| Prerequisite | Existing records/artifacts → inspectable extraction with sources → fidelity review → usable continuation | This is the import dependency, not a requirement to finish every tree before acting. Original manual pages and named collaboration sources remain gaps |
| Transition | Implement the interface; import prior work; verify the reading; **continue development through this interface** | The first three are completed within the stipulated target. The fourth is current work, not a fresh recommendation to build RC |

The intervention has crossed an important boundary: **a proposed change became
part of the operating system**. Its original reason and expectation remain
inspectable, but its old obstacle cannot remain the permanent next step. A
successful build is evidence of implementation; sustained net benefit from
using RC remains a different question.

The imported state should therefore preserve this sequence:

1. The expert and SQ develop the goal, difficulties and operating account.
2. The builders use the manual record system on RC; its detailed pages are
   missing, so only the supplied account is available.
3. Manual overhead motivates the interface injection and AI-assisted import.
4. The team builds the effective TUI (stipulated scenario event).
5. Prior work is imported and verified (stipulated target event).
6. The builders resume their work through the TUI, with new inputs, questions,
   actions and outcomes appended from here.

This is a source chronology, not six recovered application revisions. The native
commons may have one imported baseline and a real present-day import receipt.
Original authoring time, event time and import time must remain distinguishable.
Do not manufacture dated historical transactions, ownership declarations or
acceptances to make the History screen look complete.

## The first screen after import

An editable commons named **Reason Commons** opens on the continuing work. The
goal and minimality/dogfood protections are already available; the user does not
have to restate them to unlock the workspace. A small receipt says what was
imported, what the fidelity review covered and what is missing. Explanation,
trees, sources and history are local reads, close at hand.

A suitable initial question, authored now rather than claimed to be inherited,
is:

> Which decision in developing Reason Commons do you need to move forward now?

The relevant context is short: the interface was the previous intervention;
its purpose is to reduce the burden of turning reasoning into useful progress;
this commons already contains the rationale and the earlier work. An ordinary
answer, correction, observation or request for direct help continues from that
state. The consultant asks only for missing knowledge likely to change what
happens next. If a useful direct action is already clear, it can recommend it;
if another question would cost more than it adds, it can stop.

This prompt is a continuation candidate. It is not evidence that the last
historical speaker asked it, nor an instruction to diagnose RC's bottleneck
from a predetermined menu. Since the imported record contains no specific next
implementation decision, naming one here would be another invention.

## What to trim, and what to preserve

The immediate failure is the substitution of an exhibit for the operating
commons. `docs/images/story-now.png` opens a read-only Second Renaissance story,
ending with a stewarded-review recommendation. `adapters/story.py` turns an
editor's chapters into dated native inputs and auto-accepted model updates.
The story YAML acknowledges editorial interpretation, but native source and
input attribution identify the historical speakers. `build_story` also supplies
ownership and observed-evidence declarations from the editor's chapter fields.
Automatic acceptance records then name each input's historical speaker as the
decision actor (`domain/model.py`), even though `Story editor` chose the automatic
setting when building the example. That can make editorial model changes,
declarations and acceptance decisions appear to be the original participants'
transactions. It does not reconstruct the RC interface intervention required by
this scenario.

Read-only inspection of the packaged `.reasoncase` confirms the representation:
24 revisions and 294 records, with 24 automatic acceptance decisions attributed
to David Joseph (15), Robert Bunge (5), Rufus Pollock (3) and curious_reader (1).
The actor who selected automatic acceptance is `Story editor`. These are counts
in the authored fixture, not evidence those people performed the decisions.

`docs/images/tree-all-six.png` shows another substitution: a personal
open-evening goal above imported civilization-scale necessary conditions. The
sample builder rejects the file's imported goal but accepts its other tree
claims. This is useful for exercising rendering, but it is not a coherent RC
dogfood commons or evidence that those conditions serve the goal above them.

The supplied JSON is a different artifact from that story. It supplies the right
design conversation, but its invented protocol and staged human acts also need
to stay outside recovered authoritative history. We have no evidence identifying
which artifact was altered, or reconstructing David and Rufus's contributions
from it.

Preserve durable sources, exact targets, versioned reasoning, original forecasts,
reviewable proposals, corrections, local browsing and portable handoff. They
support the conversation's learning loop and make mistakes recoverable. Removing
them to shorten a menu would remove the means of checking the system's reading.

Minimality concerns the **work a person must perform to make progress**, not the
number of historical records the app is allowed to retain. A quiet archive need
not be a present backlog. Conversely, ten short mandatory interactions can cost
more than one well-chosen question. The consultant's questions, import review
and interface operations all belong in the same assessment of scarce attention.

## Scope and application fit

Most of this restoration fits the existing application boundary: source
attachment, import proposals, model admission, local explanation, immutable
history and live continuation. The repair should first change the supplied commons
and its entry path, then test one real RC work session before adding features.

There are two concrete limitations to respect:

- V1 has one goal and one operator per commons. It cannot claim David/Rufus group
  agreement or store the JSON's `STANCE` as a native ratification. Preserve such
  material as attributed source prose until an explicitly authorized later
  capability exists.
- Native loop actions and observations reference a test. The source supplies no
  bounded prospective test for the historical interface build. Its completion
  belongs in attributed historical prose/tree context; do not create a fake test
  so it can be marked completed in the loop UI. General action tracking, if
  needed, would be a separate specification decision.

No scenario or delivery tag is changed by this audit. The proposed trims are
reviewable repair targets, not claims that the TUI has already been restored.
The source copy is preserved evidence, not a replacement collaborative ledger.

The practical acceptance check for the eventual repair is one session: open
the imported RC commons, recognize its goal and the already-implemented interface
intervention, locate their sources, contribute the next real development issue,
make or revise one useful next move, leave, and resume the same work. Record the
time and effort actually incurred and whether the move was acted on. Passing a
fixture or rendering a compelling tree alone cannot establish that benefit.

quoted source spans, entry sequence, entry references, JSON syntax and the
report's artifact links were checked. The existing packaged story was inspected
read-only through `CaseApplication`; its representation is recorded in the audit.

12 tree-import cases, 16 proposal/review cases, 46 cases covering the 31 delivered
p1 scenarios, 10 other delivered p2 cases and 23 conversation scenarios. The
gate continues to list the undelivered scenarios. This establishes the current
implementation baseline; no application behavior was changed, and it does not
verify the proposed restoration or participant usability.
